Pass Tech keeps your passwords on your phone and nowhere else. No account, no
server of ours, no cloud, no tracker, no advertising.

<b>The vault</b>
* AES-256-GCM. The key comes from your master password through Argon2id
  (19 MiB of memory, 2 passes — the OWASP 2024 baseline for mobile) and from a
  key held in the phone's secure chip, which takes part in every single
  attempt: a copy of the vault carried to another machine cannot be attacked
  there.
* Your master password is never stored, in any form. It cannot be recovered —
  not by us, not by anyone.
* Five free attempts, then a wait that grows from 30 seconds to 30 minutes,
  counted in a file so it survives a restart.
* Fingerprint unlock, its key in the Android Keystore, destroyed if the
  fingerprints on the phone change.
* Locks itself when you leave it. No screenshot and no preview in the list of
  recent apps.
* Left out of Android's cloud backup and of phone-to-phone transfer: the only
  copy that exists is the one you make.

<b>If someone makes you open it</b>
* Decoy vault: a second password opens a second vault with its own entries, and
  nothing on the phone says whether you have one.
* Panic mode: locks, clears the clipboard, disarms the fingerprint, withdraws
  the address check and turns the app into a calculator that really works.
  Nothing is deleted.
* Heir access: someone you choose opens a read-only snapshot of your vault
  after a long enough silence on your side. On the phone, with no cloud and no
  third party.

<b>What it holds</b>
Passwords, bank cards and secure notes. Two-factor codes generated here, from a
pasted otpauth:// link. Search and sort. A generator for passwords, and for
passphrases drawn from a word list in the language the app is showing.

<b>What it watches for you</b>
* A security audit of the vault: what is weak, what is reused, what has no
  second factor.
* A breach check you start yourself, which never sends the password — only the
  first five characters of its fingerprint, shared by tens of thousands of
  others.
* An address check: before a password is copied, it compares the site open in
  your browser with the ones the entry declares, and holds the copy back when
  none of them matches. It uses an Android accessibility service that is off
  until you ask for it, reads the address bar of the browsers it knows and
  nothing else, and keeps what it reads in memory for fifteen seconds.

<b>The two times it uses the network</b>
Asking GitHub whether a newer version has been published, at most twice a day,
and the breach check above. Neither carries anything you typed, and both stop
entirely while the app is disguised.

<b>Languages</b>
English, French, German, Italian and Spanish, picked in the app whatever the
phone is set to.

<b>Open source</b>
Apache License 2.0. The whole source is on GitHub, and every release publishes
the SHA-256 of each file it carries, so you can check that what you installed
is what was published.
